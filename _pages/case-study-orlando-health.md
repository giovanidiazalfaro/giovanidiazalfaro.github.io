## The problem as it was handed to me

Errors were showing up at every stage of the imaging centers' work. The operational data could tell me where errors landed, but not why they happened, and without the why, any fix would have been a guess.

## The choice I made, and why

I paired the numbers with time spent watching the work. Each method covered something the others could not.

<div class="cs-table-wrap">
<table class="cs-table">
  <thead><tr><th scope="col">Method</th><th scope="col">What it was for</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Operational data analysis</td><td>Compared error and productivity data against internal and external benchmarks to locate the risk points worth studying. Done in Excel.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Task analysis</td><td>Mapped clinical and non-clinical workflows step by step, so each error could be tied to a specific point in the work.</td></tr>

    <tr><td>Surveys and focus groups</td><td>Collected workflow and process feedback and training needs from staff.</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

## What changed

I defined performance metrics and led the process redesign. That meant standardized workflows with written guides, staff retraining, staffing and schedule changes, and ongoing audits with regular reporting on productivity, error, and revenue metrics. I presented the findings to stakeholders to build support for each change.

## Epic EHR launch

When the organization moved to Epic, I managed training, built the launch reference resources, and ran usability testing on the EHR.

## What's still open

The results are a before and after comparison. The changes went in together, so I can't say how much each one contributed on its own. With more time I would have staged them to find out.
